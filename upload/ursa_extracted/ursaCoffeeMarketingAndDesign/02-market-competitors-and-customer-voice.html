Module 02 · Market, Competitors & Customer Voice

The Miraflores specialty scene is crowded. Ursa's distinction is real but under-told.

This module maps the direct Miraflores specialty-coffee competitors and Lima-wide leaders, extracts the recurring patterns in customer reviews, and converts them into five categories: standards Ursa must match, problems to avoid, areas where Ursa can lead, customer-experience improvements, and product opportunities. The sample is balanced and dated; no review is fabricated.

Direct competitors analyzed · 11 Miraflores cafés Lima leaders benchmarked · 3 (Bisetti, Ciclos, RAIZ) Customer-voice source · TripAdvisor + third-party guides + curated press Snapshot · 2026-08-01

Where Ursa sits in this set

Ursa is one of the smaller-in-scale, roastery-first, identity-rich cafés in Miraflores. Its closest analog is Arabica Espresso & Slow Bar (dual-bar concept) and, in spirit, Neira Café Lab (roastery + authority). Where Ursa is meaningfully different is in brand identity: the bear, the tagline, the named-portmanteau drinks, and the Art Nouveau lean. None of the eleven competitors has a comparable character-led identity. This is the leverage point — provided distribution and review-channel gaps are closed.

2.2

Customer-voice patterns

Ursa's TripAdvisor listing currently shows zero reviews, so direct Ursa customer voice cannot be reconstructed from review channels. Instead, the customer-voice analysis below is built from competitor reviews and specialty-coffee guide commentary. Patterns that recur across multiple competitors and multiple reviewers are treated as reliable; single-reviewer complaints are flagged as anecdotal. No review is fabricated.

What customers say competitors do exceptionally well

  • "Serious and fun" — Origen tostadores de café is praised for balancing price, service, and quality without taking itself too seriously. The phrase recurs across reviews.
  • Equipment-as-credibility — Neira Café Lab's La Marzocco machine is mentioned unprompted in reviews as a quality signal. Equipment visible from the bar functions as proof.
  • Founder authority — Harrysson Neira's barista-champion title is referenced in third-party guides and customer reviews as a reason to visit. Founder authority shortens the trust journey.
  • Atmosphere as destination — Puku Puku's "cozy" descriptor recurs. Tostaduría Bisetti's "beautiful old building" is described as a reason to visit independent of the coffee.
  • Service closeness — Neira reviews mention "atención cercana" (close/friendly service) as a positive. The barista-customer interaction is itself a product.
  • Peruvian-origin pride — Multiple reviewers across competitors describe pride in drinking high-quality Peruvian coffee. The category itself is a recruitment vehicle.

What customers say competitors do poorly

  • Cost/aroma/acidity mismatch — A negative Origen review specifically calls out that "Cost, Aroma and acidity levels are not very pleasant in our opinion" and that "service could be more qualified." The reviewer is essentially saying: when the cup disappoints, no atmosphere rescues it.
  • Simple dessert selection — A Neira review notes "Hay variedad de postres, aunque bastante simples" (variety of desserts, but quite simple). Food program weakness undercuts the average ticket.
  • Not for laptops / quick visits — Neira is described as "not a place for a quick latte and laptop session." This is positioning, but it also limits the use-case base.
  • Service qualification gaps — The same Origen review calls service qualification into question. Specialty customers expect knowledgeable staff; gaps are noticed.

What customers say competitors fail to make clear

  • Origin and producer information — When reviewers praise coffee knowledge, they often mention wanting more visible origin detail. Many cafés print origin on chalkboards but not on the menu.
  • What makes a "specialty" café specialty — Guides repeatedly have to explain the distinction to readers. The category itself is opaque to first-time customers.
  • Brew-method recommendations per bean — Reviewers across multiple cafés ask which method suits which bean. Default recommendations are rarely visible.
  • Class and cupping calendars — Education offerings, when they exist, are often hidden inside Instagram posts and hard to find later.

What customers find difficult or frustrating

  • Seating at peak hours — Common across Miraflores cafés. Small footprint + high traffic = waitlists or awkward standing.
  • Distinguishing one specialty café from another — Guides group them; customers struggle to articulate why they prefer one over another. Differentiation is the category's collective weakness.
  • Deciding what to order first — Long single-origin menus without guidance overwhelm first-time visitors.
  • Bean purchase decisions — Without brew-method guidance and origin story, retail bean choice becomes a guess.

What competitors do inconsistently

  • Cup quality across baristas and shifts — Implicit in reviewer language ("best coffee in Lima" vs "service could be more qualified" for the same café). Specialty customers notice variance.
  • Food and pastry quality — When cafés buy in pastries, reviewers notice. When pastry is in-house, it is praised. Inconsistency reads as a brand failure.
  • Operating hours vs delivery hours — Rappi listing for Ursa shows delivery hours (08:00–20:30) shorter than in-store hours (07:30–21:00). This kind of gap is common across the category and erodes delivery conversion.

What customers value strongly

  • Visible craft — Pouring, weighing, grinding in front of the customer. The performance of craft is part of the product.
  • Knowledgeable, non-condescending staff — The single most praised attribute across the set.
  • Peruvian origin pride with global technique — Customers want local origin and international execution in the same cup.
  • Atmosphere that earns staying — Not just "cozy" but a place where staying 30 minutes feels appropriate, not transactional.
  • House-made food — Pastries and savory items made on-site are explicitly praised and distinguish cafés from buy-in competitors.

Sources: Neira TripAdvisor reviews, Origen TripAdvisor reviews, Curious Spoon Neira profile, Curious Spoon Bisetti profile, World's 100 Best — Neira, World's 100 Best — Punto Café. Snapshot 2026-08-01.

Areas where Ursa can lead

Identity-led brand character

None of the eleven competitors has a comparable character-led identity (bear + tagline + named-portmanteau drinks + Art Nouveau lean). Ursa can lead by making the bear a recurring content character, the tagline a system-level sign-off, and the two-bar concept a permanent identity asset rather than a promotion.

Two-bar architecture as positioning

Only Arabica Espresso & Slow Bar comes close to a dual-bar concept. Ursa's espresso bar + coldbrew bar is operationally and narratively distinctive. Lead with paired-motif menus, dual-column signage, and "barra espresso / barra coldbrew" labels in store.

Named-portmanteau beverage program

Ursagroni and Maracumango are ownable names. Build a documented naming convention (Ursa- + flavor / origin / method) and release one named beverage per month. Competitors' drink lists are generic by comparison.

In-house food storytelling

Financier de pera, empanada de carne con bechamel, and house-made cookies are already differentiated. Lead with named pastries, paired to specific coffees on the menu, and visible in content.

Tourism and hospitality pipeline

No competitor appears to have a structured hotel-concierge sampling program. With 8+ hotels within 6 blocks of Alcanfores 183, Ursa can lead a Miraflores café-tourism pipeline that others will have to catch up to.

Documented cupping and education calendar

Cate Tasting Room is the closest competitor on education. Most others are ad hoc. A regular Saturday cupping at the bar (EXP-03 in experiments.json) is low-cost and differentiated.

Customer-experience improvements

  • First-visit decision aid — A 1-page "Si es tu primera vez" (If it's your first time) table card with three recommended orders by mood (warm, cold, sweet) reduces ordering anxiety and speeds the line.
  • Bean decision aid — A bean-card system on the retail shelf (origin, altitude, process, suggested method, pairing) closes the "bean purchase decision" gap. See EXP-02.
  • Two-bar wayfinding — Sign each bar with its name and a one-line description of what each does best. Helps first-time visitors orient.
  • Class calendar pin — Permanent in-store chalkboard listing the next two cuppings or classes. Mirrors digital pinning.
  • Service-recovery card — A small card given with the bill: "Si algo no estuvo bien, cuéntanos aquí." with WhatsApp. Catches dissatisfaction before it becomes a negative review.

Product opportunities

  • "Doble Barra Ursa" first-visit bundle — one espresso-based + one coldbrew + one house pastry at a bundled price. See EXP-01. Closes the "what to order first" gap.
  • Named seasonal beverage per month — Following the Ursagroni / Maracumango convention. Each drop gets a 60-second Reel and a story-card.
  • Home-brew starter kit — V60 or Aeropress + 250g bean + recipe card + access to a 5-minute WhatsApp video. Closes the "brew-method recommendations" gap and creates a higher-ticket retail item.
  • Hotel concierge sampling kit — Sample bag + "Breakfast Coffee Guide for Guests" + unique QR per hotel. See EXP-05.
  • Office subscription pilot — Bean + filter loan + weekly check-in for Miraflores offices. See EXP-08.
  • Bean "drop" WhatsApp notify list — 24-hour early access to new single origins. See EXP-06.

Marketing proof points

  • "Tostadores de café de especialidad" — verified bio claim. Usable in ads, partnerships, and PR.
  • "Un gramo a la vez" — verified tagline. A proof point for precision and patience.
  • Two-bar layout — verified operational distinction. Usable as a positioning proof point.
  • Named single origins — Lonya, Utcubamba, Amazonas is verified. Usable in tourism and B2B materials.
  • In-house food program — verified. Usable as a quality proof point versus buy-in competitors.
  • Owner-supplied (pending) — Art Nouveau visual language, bear motif application, brown-green palette. Becomes a proof point once visually verified via asset pack (open question Q6).

2.6

Skeptical revision pass

Skeptic 1

"You built customer voice from competitor reviews because Ursa has none. Isn't that unreliable?"

It is a deliberate limitation, not a hidden one. The dossier names it explicitly in §2.2. Competitor reviews describe the shared category expectations of Miraflores specialty customers; they do not describe Ursa-specific experiences. The plan therefore treats competitor findings as a baseline Ursa must meet, and uses Ursa's own Instagram and Facebook engagement as the first signal of Ursa-specific customer voice. The first three live experiments (EXP-01, EXP-03, EXP-05) are designed to surface real Ursa-specific voice within 30 days.

Skeptic 2

"The competitive set is missing newer entrants. Are you sure?"

The set is built from the most recent third-party guides (Barista Magazine, Practical Wanderlust, Tales from the Lens, Curious Spoon, The World's 100 Best Coffee Shops) and TripAdvisor's current Miraflores list. Newer entrants that have not yet earned guide mention or significant review volume are unlikely to be recruiting Ursa's target customers today. The set is re-verified quarterly in the roadmap (§07).

Skeptic 3

"Punto Café and Puku Puku are World's 100 Best nominees. Shouldn't Ursa aim for that too?"

No, not as a primary objective. The World's 100 Best list rewards scale, marketing spend, and tourism visibility — none of which are Ursa's current strengths. Pursuing the nomination would distort the plan toward a single vanity metric. Instead, the plan focuses on the underlying assets that produce such nominations (review volume, customer love, distinctive experience). If a nomination follows, it follows.
