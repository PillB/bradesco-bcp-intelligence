Module 06 · Creative Campaign Prototypes

Prototypes you can click, copy, and hand to a printer.

This module builds working HTML, CSS, and SVG prototypes for fifteen touchpoints — social posts, carousels, Stories, Reel covers, posters, menus, product cards, table signs, loyalty cards, packaging labels, bean info cards, event materials, Google and delivery imagery, landing pages, and email headers. The official logo is never altered or counterfeited; the geometric bear glyph used here is an original concept mark for prototype demonstration only.

Prototypes · 15 touchpoints Format · HTML + CSS + SVG Brand evolution · 3 levels with recommendation Print-ready · Yes

Asset disclaimer — read before any production

The geometric bear glyph used throughout this module is an original concept mark designed for dossier navigation and prototype demonstration. It is not the official Ursa Coffee Roasters logo. The official logo must be sourced from the owner-supplied asset pack (open question Q6 in Sources & Evidence) before any production artwork is finalized. All prototypes below use the concept mark as a placeholder; in production, the official mark replaces it. No prototype in this module alters, parodies, or counterfeits the official logo.

Recommendation — confirmed

Level 1 as the permanent system, Level 2 as a growth skin from month 4, Level 3 only as seasonal expression. The visual comparison makes the reason clear: Level 1 protects identity at zero risk, Level 2 makes Ursa unmistakable at thumbnail size (where 90% of social impressions happen), and Level 3 stretches the system for limited periods without breaking it. Skipping Level 1 in pursuit of Level 2 is the single most common reason rebrands fail; this plan does not skip.

6.14

Skeptical revision pass

Skeptic 1

"These are HTML prototypes, not production files. How does the owner use them?"

Each prototype is print-ready via the browser's print-to-PDF function (validated in §6.15). For social posts and Stories, screenshots at 2x resolution produce production-grade JPEGs. For packaging and printed materials, the HTML/CSS serves as a precise spec for any designer or printer — exact colors (hex), fonts (named), and dimensions (aspect ratios) are all in the source. The owner can hand a screenshot to a printer and say "match this".

Skeptic 2

"You used a generic bear in the prototypes. The real bear might look totally different."

Correct — and stated explicitly in the asset disclaimer at the top of this module. The geometric bear used here is a placeholder concept mark for navigation and prototype demonstration. The owner-supplied asset pack (open question Q6) is required before any production artwork is finalized. Once the official bear is sourced, it replaces the concept mark in every prototype without changing the layout, palette, or typography.

Skeptic 3

"Level 3 with inverted constellation and numbered editions is too cute for a small café."

Fair concern — which is why Level 3 is restricted to seasonal expression and explicitly recommended as optional. If the owner finds it too cute, the plan degrades gracefully: Level 1 + Level 2 alone produce a complete, distinctive system. Level 3 exists for the moment when the owner wants to celebrate a season, an anniversary, or a limited origin — never as a permanent register.
