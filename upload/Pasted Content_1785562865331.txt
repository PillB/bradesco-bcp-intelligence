## RESEARCH GOVERNANCE AND SOURCE HIERARCHY

Research must be reproducible, discriminating, and proportionate to the claim. Do not equate the number of links with quality.

Use this source hierarchy, adapted to each domain:

1. Ursa first-party assets, owner-supplied operational data, current official menus, POS data, campaign results, customer databases, and direct observations.
2. Primary research: peer-reviewed experiments, field experiments, randomized studies, validated surveys, original datasets, official platform documentation, regulatory or standards documents, and original expert publications.
3. Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, consensus reports, scholarly handbooks, and authoritative institutional guidance.
4. High-quality observational studies, industry datasets with transparent methodology, and reputable market research.
5. Credible expert analysis that clearly distinguishes evidence from opinion.
6. Reputable journalism and trade publications for current events, examples, or industry context.
7. Direct competitor websites, menus, social accounts, map listings, delivery platforms, and public reviews for contemporaneous market observation.
8. Forums, Reddit, creator posts, and social commentary only for hypothesis generation, vocabulary discovery, complaints, or emerging behavior—not as final proof of effectiveness.

Hard source rules:

* Search snippets are discovery tools, not evidence.
* One original claim copied across several sites is one source family, not independent corroboration.
* Prefer the newest reliable evidence for current platform, price, product, competitor, regulation, or market claims.
* Retain seminal older research when it remains foundational, but distinguish it from current evidence.
* Seek disconfirming and null evidence deliberately.
* Record publication date, event date, retrieval date, jurisdiction, population, setting, method, sample, effect estimate where available, limitations, conflicts of interest, and directness to Ursa.
* Do not use a study of laboratory attention or neural response as proof of real-world sales, loyalty, or profitability.
* Do not infer mental states from brain-region activation without validated interpretation.
* Do not treat correlation, stated preference, engagement, recall, purchase intent, and actual purchase as equivalent outcomes.
* Do not convert an expert’s memorable framework into a scientific law.

## PREPONDERANCE-OF-EVIDENCE MODEL

Create a canonical claim ledger in `research/claim-ledger.json` and a readable HTML view. Every material claim must be represented once and referenced everywhere else by claim ID.

Use this record:

```json
{
  "claim_id": "CLM-0001",
  "claim_text": "bounded, inspectable claim",
  "claim_domain": "brand|design|marketing|sales|consumer-psychology|neuroscience|customer|competitor|market|menu|operations|finance|platform|accessibility|other",
  "claim_type": "descriptive|comparative|causal|forecast|normative|financial|operational|interpretive",
  "artifact_locations": [],
  "sources_supporting": [],
  "sources_challenging": [],
  "source_families": [],
  "evidence_quality": "high|moderate|low|very_low|not_assessable",
  "directness_to_ursa": "direct|near|indirect|remote",
  "consistency": "consistent|mostly_consistent|mixed|contradictory|unknown",
  "independence": "high|moderate|low",
  "recency": "current|recent|dated|timeless_or_seminal|unknown",
  "effect_size_or_magnitude": "value, range, qualitative magnitude, or unavailable",
  "uncertainty": "explicit uncertainty statement",
  "preponderance_grade": "A|B|C|D|F",
  "status": "verified|supported|provisional|contested|unsupported|retracted",
  "decision": "retain|narrow|qualify|replace|remove|test",
  "last_reviewed": "ISO date",
  "review_notes": []
}
```

Grade claims as follows:

* **A — Strong:** multiple independent high-quality sources; direct or near-direct relevance; materially consistent findings; important limitations understood.
* **B — Moderate:** credible evidence with some indirectness, heterogeneity, recency, or methodological limitations.
* **C — Suggestive:** plausible and supported by limited, observational, context-specific, or expert evidence; suitable for a low-risk test, not a confident public claim.
* **D — Insufficient or conflicting:** evidence too weak, contradictory, stale, or indirect for a recommendation without additional validation.
* **F — Unsupported or refuted:** no adequate support, serious misrepresentation, or evidence points against the claim.

Public wording must match the grade. Grade C cannot be written as fact. Grade D must be framed as an open question or removed. Grade F must be removed or explicitly corrected.

## SIX DISTINCT RESEARCH ROUNDS PER MACROCYCLE

Complete six research rounds in every macrocycle. These are not six repetitions of the same web search. Each round has a separate epistemic purpose, method, output, and failure test.

### Research Round 1 — Claim Forensics and Source Reconstruction

Purpose: determine what the current website says, where each claim originated, and whether the cited material supports it.

Actions:

* Extract every material claim from all static HTML reports, Next.js views, tools, calculators, structured data, charts, tooltips, captions, headlines, and calls to action.
* Link duplicates to one canonical claim ID.
* Open and inspect every cited source rather than relying on snippets or prior summaries.
* Identify citation drift, scope inflation, outdated facts, circular sourcing, broken links, and unsupported confidence.
* Compare claim wording with source wording without copying copyrighted text.
* Verify all numerical calculations and denominators.

Round output:

* Updated claim ledger.
* Source-family map.
* Unsupported-claim list.
* Contradiction list.
* Stale-current-fact list.

Failure test: no high-impact existing claim may remain without a recorded source assessment.

### Research Round 2 — Theory, Methodology, and Scientific Evidence

Purpose: validate the intellectual foundations of each analysis and recommendation.

Research, as relevant:

* Marketing science and brand growth.
* Positioning, category entry points, mental and physical availability, distinctive assets, creative effectiveness, pricing, promotions, loyalty, retention, referral, word of mouth, local search, and hospitality marketing.
* Sales and offer design, with attention to unit economics, ethics, consumer protection, and boundary conditions.
* Consumer psychology, behavioral economics, decision science, habit, memory, attention, affect, social proof, identity, trust, perceived risk, defaults, framing, scarcity, anchoring, choice architecture, and experience design.
* Consumer neuroscience only where it adds validated incremental insight beyond behavioral measures.
* Design research: visual hierarchy, typography, color, legibility, brand recognition, packaging, environmental graphics, accessibility, cross-cultural interpretation, and information design.
* Market-research methodology: sampling, review mining, qualitative coding, survey design, segmentation, interview bias, social-listening limitations, and triangulation.
* Causal inference, experimental design, measurement validity, statistical power, heterogeneous effects, external validity, replication, and publication bias.
* Hospitality operations, queueing, service recovery, menu engineering, food safety, throughput, inventory, waste, pricing, and contribution margin.

For every framework used, record:

* Original proposition.
* Best supporting evidence.
* Best challenging evidence.
* Populations and settings studied.
* Whether outcomes were attention, memory, preference, intent, behavior, sales, profit, or retention.
* Effect magnitude and uncertainty where available.
* Conditions under which the effect weakens, reverses, or becomes unethical.
* Direct applicability to a small specialty café in Miraflores.
* What the Ursa recommendation may legitimately claim.

Failure test: no named theory, guru, principle, neuroscience concept, or psychological mechanism may remain merely because it sounds persuasive.

### Research Round 3 — Ursa, Client, Brand, Product, and Operational Reality

Purpose: ground external theory in the actual business.

Actions:

* Revisit current official Ursa accounts, menus, delivery listings, map profiles, visual assets, product posts, events, partnerships, reviews, and public business information.
* Distinguish current from historical products and promotions.
* Request one grouped owner-data package only when the public pass cannot resolve material questions. Do not ask repeatedly.
* When owner data is unavailable, use transparent scenario ranges and mark decisions provisional.
* Analyze brand assets at the image/source level. Separate observed palette and type characteristics from official specifications and proposed substitutes.
* Validate menu availability, pricing, ingredient claims, preparation methods, hours, locations, and product names.
* Audit operational plausibility: equipment, bar workflow, staffing, queueing, training, ingredient overlap, waste, shelf life, delivery stability, food safety, and peak capacity.
* Audit financial plausibility: actual or scenario contribution margin, cannibalization, attach rate, visit frequency, capacity constraints, break-even, and downside risk.

Failure test: external theory may not override direct Ursa evidence or operational reality.

### Research Round 4 — Customer, Competitor, Market, and Expert Field Evidence

Purpose: replace impressionistic market analysis with a documented empirical method.

Actions:

* Define the geographic and category boundaries before selecting competitors.
* Verify that each competitor is currently operating and relevant.
* Build direct-local, citywide-specialty, indirect-alternative, and aspirational groups.
* Record menu, price, product, positioning, brand, channel, review, service, accessibility, delivery, event, education, B2B, loyalty, and local-search evidence with dates.
* Conduct review and social-comment sampling with a documented period, platform mix, inclusion/exclusion rules, deduplication, language handling, and actual sample count.
* Preserve both praise and criticism.
* Code review-level observations using a versioned codebook.
* Separate frequency from salience and isolated anecdotes from repeated themes.
* Where feasible, use two independent coders or an independent audit sample and record disagreements.
* Do not attribute a competitor weakness without sufficient direct evidence.
* Conduct expert research using verifiable credentials and disclosed perspective. Separate expert opinion from empirical evidence.
* Identify customer jobs, anxieties, vocabulary, switching triggers, and unmet needs from evidence, not decorative personas.

Required outputs:

* `research/competitor-observations.json`
* `research/review-sample.json`
* `research/customer-codebook.md`
* `research/customer-theme-matrix.csv`
* `research/expert-evidence.json`

Failure test: every reported customer or competitor theme must be traceable to the underlying sample and have a prevalence or strength label.

### Research Round 5 — Feasibility, Economics, Risk, Ethics, and Implementation Science

Purpose: test whether the recommendations can work responsibly in practice.

For every major recommendation, assess:

* Required owner and staff time.
* Training and process change.
* Equipment, software, creative, media, production, and opportunity costs.
* Capacity, throughput, waste, failure demand, seasonality, and supply risk.
* Contribution margin and downside scenarios.
* Cannibalization and adverse selection.
* Customer confusion, fairness, accessibility, privacy, consent, labor, food-safety, consumer-protection, licensing, copyright, and platform-policy concerns.
* Dependency on third parties.
* Reversibility and cost of failure.
* Minimum viable test.
* Measurement instrumentation.
* Success, guardrail, and stopping criteria.
* Conditions for scale, iteration, pause, or rejection.

Use an implementation-confidence score that is separate from scientific evidence strength.

Failure test: no recommendation may be labeled “adopt now” unless both evidence and implementation confidence are adequate.

### Research Round 6 — Falsification, Replication, Synthesis, and Research Saturation

Purpose: attempt to disprove the current conclusions before improving the website.

Actions:

* Search specifically for opposing findings, failed replications, adverse effects, alternative explanations, and competitor counterexamples.
* Recalculate important figures independently.
* Re-open the strongest sources and verify that the summaries remain accurate.
* Check whether multiple recommendations rest on the same weak assumption.
* Test sensitivity to different customer segments, time horizons, prices, costs, effect sizes, and operational constraints.
* Identify where evidence is saturated and where uncertainty remains irreducible without first-party field data.
* Produce a synthesis that gives more weight to quality, directness, consistency, and independence than to source count.
* Narrow or remove recommendations that do not survive falsification.

Failure test: the round must produce either a meaningful challenge, a stronger bounded conclusion, or documented saturation. “No issues found” without an inspectable search and challenge record is invalid.

* Mobile behavior.
* Saved-state behavior.
* Clear labels and units.
* Honest uncertainty.
* No fabricated data disguised as live intelligence.
* No output that exceeds what the input evidence supports.

## HANDCRAFTED WRITING AND EDITORIAL QUALITY PROTOCOL — HARD GATE

Use the uploaded protocol as binding editorial governance.

For each meaningful writing unit:

1. Diagnose its current purpose, strengths, failures, omissions, redundancies, and reader risk.
2. Research the facts, terminology, examples, and relevant evidence.
3. Create a compact unit brief containing audience, purpose, reader starting point, core question, required outcome, key claims, supporting and challenging evidence, limitations, likely misunderstandings, connections backward and forward, tone, and risks.
4. Draft attentively for that exact unit.
5. Perform substantive editing for logic, completeness, evidence, distinctions, trade-offs, and usefulness.
6. Perform a style and redaction pass for clarity, cadence, accessible sophistication, warmth, precision, paragraph balance, and productive concision.
7. Perform an anti-aberration pass for generic openings, repetitive structures, interchangeable prose, jargon, unsupported confidence, topic contamination, invented facts, and meta-language.
8. Perform reader validation.
9. Inspect the rendered result.
10. Record sources, decisions, corrected defects, rejected approaches, and residual uncertainty.

Do not mass-produce final prose with loops, paragraph templates, substitution systems, or unattended bulk generation. Automation may inventory, compare, validate, calculate, detect duplication, check links, and run tests. It may not replace unit-level authorship and review.

Every final public-facing unit must pass:

* Purpose.
* Structure.
* Substance.
* Readability.
* Voice.
* Original authorship quality.
* Reliability.

Minimum gate: 18/21; no category below 2; Substance, Original Authorship Quality, and Reliability must each score 3.

## SOFTWARE AND DATA ARCHITECTURE REQUIREMENTS

Prevent future divergence between the static dossier and the Next.js application.

1. Establish a canonical structured-data layer for claims, sources, evidence statuses, competitors, products, recommendations, experiments, and financial assumptions.
2. Do not duplicate volatile facts manually across many components when they can safely reference canonical data.
3. Do not generate final prose automatically from generic templates. Use canonical data for factual consistency while preserving handcrafted report prose.
4. Add schema validation for all structured files.
5. Add tests that fail when:

   * Reported evidence counts differ from JSON counts.
   * Experiment counts differ.
   * Claim IDs are missing or duplicated.
   * A public “verified” claim lacks qualifying evidence.
   * A cited URL is malformed.
   * Current-fact records lack retrieval dates.
   * Calculators produce non-finite or inconsistent results.
   * Static and interactive versions disagree on canonical facts.
6. Preserve accessibility and reduced-motion behavior.
7. Remove dead files only when confirmed unused; do not delete research provenance.
8. Keep research artifacts separate from public production assets unless they are deliberately published.

## VALIDATION REQUIREMENTS

### Research Validation

* Verify dates, units, denominators, formulas, and claim scope.
* Confirm that cited evidence supports the text beside it.

### Editorial Validation

* Compare adjacent and recent sections for repetition.
* Remove formulaic introductions and conclusions.
* Remove internal process language from public pages.
* Explain specialized terms in context.
* Bound causal and absolute language.
* Ensure every paragraph has a clear function.

### Code and Data Validation

Run the project’s appropriate commands, including at minimum:

* Dependency installation using the repository’s package manager.
* Lint.
* Type checking.
* Structured-data/schema validation.
* Unit/calculation tests.

Do not ignore pre-existing failures. Determine whether they are genuinely pre-existing, whether they affect the changed work, and whether they should be fixed to meet the current Definition of Done.

### Browser and Rendered Validation

Using Playwright or the available browser tooling:

* Test every route and primary navigation path.
* Test all buttons, tabs, filters, accordions, sliders, calculators, export or print actions, theme controls, language controls, and persistent-state behaviors.

* Test representative screen widths around 375, 768, and 1440 pixels.

* Verify that mobile tables, charts, and long evidence records remain usable.

## HARD-FAILURE CONDITIONS

Reject the release when any of the following remains:

* Fabricated evidence, reviews, data, quotations, credentials, or research sample sizes.
* Wrong business, location, or competitor identity.
* A source does not support the cited claim.
* A current fact lacks a retrieval date.
* A material causal claim is based only on correlation, opinion, or laboratory proxy.
* Neuroscience is used to overclaim sales or psychological certainty.
* Customer voice lacks a documented sample and method.
* Exact brand specifications are invented.
* Public reports and structured data contradict one another.
* Financial tools imply validation from illustrative assumptions.
* “Best,” “proven,” “guaranteed,” “category-first,” or “no competitor” claims lack adequate search and qualification.
* Final prose is bulk-generated, formulaic, or substantially interchangeable.
* Internal prompts, agent language, audit notes, or hidden processes leak into public pages.
* Placeholders, TODOs, fake metrics, empty sections, broken links, or broken routes remain.
* The app does not build.
* Critical interactions fail.
* The rendered result has not been inspected.
* The authoring agent is the sole validator.
* File existence or screenshot existence is treated as proof of quality.
* The final response claims perfection rather than bounded validation.
