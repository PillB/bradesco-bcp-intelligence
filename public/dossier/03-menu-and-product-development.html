Module 03 · Menu & Product Development

Reconstruct the menu, then build out from the two bars.

This module reconstructs Ursa's current menu from verified sources (Instagram, Facebook, Rappi, CoffeePass), proposes new products across thirteen categories, and evaluates every proposal against sixteen operational criteria. Each proposal has a test method, a success metric, a stop rule, and a scenario price range. No exact costs are invented; where actual costs are unknown, the plan uses explicit lean / moderate / growth ranges in PEN.

What the verified menu already tells us

  • Ursa operates a real two-bar architecture (espresso bar + coldbrew bar). This is the menu's organizing principle.
  • Ursa has an ownable naming convention for distinctive drinks (Ursagroni, Maracumango, Durazno Clarificado Coldbrew). The convention is "Ursa + portmanteau" or "Fruit + Clarificado/Coldbrew".
  • Ursa pairs drinks with food deliberately (Ursagroni + empanada de carne con bechamel; Filtrado Lonya + financier de pera). Pairing is already part of the menu language.
  • Seasonal drinks graduate to permanent menu ("Las bebidas favoritas del verano se quedan en carta"). There is an established pipeline from seasonal test → permanent staple.
  • Food is positioned as in-house and crafted, not bought in.
3.2

Menu architecture proposal

The proposed menu architecture preserves the two-bar structure and adds three permanent sections (Pairings, Retail & Brew, Experiences) and one seasonal section. Each section has a clear job: drinks to order, food to add, retail to take home, experiences to return for, seasonal to drive variety.

Permanent

Barra Espresso

  • Espresso · Cortado · Flat White · Cappuccino · Latte · Americano · Mocha
  • Black Label single-origin espresso (rotating)
  • Ursagroni (signature — espresso-toned cocktail)
Permanent

Barra Coldbrew

  • Coldbrew (classic, on tap)
  • Durazno Clarificado Coldbrew
  • Maracumango Coldbrew
  • Coldbrew tonic (sparkling variation)
  • Seasonal coldbrew (one rotating)
Permanent

Filtrados

  • V60 — rotating single origin
  • Chemex — for two (sharing format)
  • Aeropress — single cup
  • Filtrado Lonya, Utcubamba, Amazonas (anchor origin)
Permanent

Dulce (sweet)

  • Financier de pera (signature)
  • House-made cookies (rotating flavors)
  • Seasonal tart or cake (one)
  • Chocolate from a verified Peruvian maker
Permanent

Salado (savory)

  • Empanada de carne con bechamel (signature)
  • One seasonal savory (e.g., hongos + queso de cabra toast)
  • One all-day option (e.g., pan con queso, jamón)
Permanent

Pairings

  • 3 documented pairings, always on menu:
    • Ursagroni + empanada de carne con bechamel
    • Filtrado Lonya + financier de pera
    • Maracumango Coldbrew + cookie de cacao
  • Pairing price < S/. sum of individual items
Permanent

Retail & Brew

  • Whole-bean single origins (3–5)
  • Home-brew starter kit (V60 or Aeropress + 250g + recipe card)
  • Branded mug
  • Branded tote
  • Filter papers, kettles (low stock)
Permanent

Experiences

  • Saturday cupping at the bar (free, 20 min, capped 6)
  • Private cupping (paid, groups of 4–8)
  • Home-brew class (paid, monthly)
  • "Roaster for an hour" (paid, by appointment)
Seasonal

Estación (Seasonal)

  • 1–2 seasonal beverages (named per Ursa convention)
  • 1 seasonal pastry
  • 1 seasonal savory
  • Limited-edition bean bag (numbered, dated)
  • Rotates every 6–8 weeks; "summer favorites" can graduate to permanent
3.3

New product proposals — evaluated against 16 criteria

Each proposal below is evaluated against sixteen criteria: customer need, brand fit, taste concept, preparation time, ingredient overlap, training, equipment, waste, delivery suitability, visual appeal, price range (lean/moderate/growth in PEN), margin potential, operational complexity, test method, success metric, and stop/revision rule. The full evaluation is condensed in tables for readability; the most important criteria (test, metric, stop) are spelled out in prose.

P-01 · Doble Barra Ursa (first-visit bundle)

Concept — A first-visit bundle: one espresso-bar drink + one coldbrew-bar drink + one house pastry, at a bundled price below the sum of individual items. Designed to convert first-time visitors into multi-product buyers and to express the two-bar identity in a single transaction.

CriterionAssessment
Customer needFirst-time visitors are unsure what to order. Bundle removes decision friction and showcases both bars.
Brand fitPerfect — bundle operationalizes the two-bar identity.
Taste conceptHot + cold + sweet in one tray; balanced palate experience.
Preparation time~3 minutes (parallel prep at both bars).
Ingredient overlapHigh — uses existing SKUs.
TrainingMinimal — staff already prepare all three.
EquipmentNone new.
WasteNone incremental.
Delivery suitabilityMedium — espresso quality degrades in transit; bundle best for in-store.
Visual appealHigh — three-item tray is photogenic and on-brand.
Price range (PEN)Lean S/. 22 · Moderate S/. 26 · Growth S/. 30 (vs S/. 28–34 unbundled)
Margin potentialMedium — bundle discounts 15–20% off sum, but increases attach rate.
Operational complexityLow — codify as a single POS button.
Test methodEXP-01 in experiments.json — 14-day pilot with one Instagram Story series and one table card.
Success metricFirst-ticket average ≥ S/. 22; ≥25% of bundle buyers return within 30 days.
Stop / revision ruleIf after 7 days first-ticket value is below S/. 18 OR fewer than 8 bundles per day are sold, kill the bundle and revert to 20% off promotion.

CriterionAssessment
Customer needCustomers want cold, low-sugar, all-day options. Tonic extends the coldbrew occasion past morning.
Brand fitHigh — extends the coldbrew bar; can be named per Ursa convention (e.g., Ursatonic).
Taste conceptBitter-sweet, effervescent; citrus peel adds aroma.
Preparation time90 seconds.
Ingredient overlapHigh — coldbrew already on tap; tonic is the only new SKU.
TrainingMinimal — pour, top, garnish.
EquipmentTonic bottle opener; bar spoon.
WasteLow — tonic bottles are returnable/recyclable.
Delivery suitabilityMedium — tonic goes flat in transit; deliver components separately.
Visual appealVery high — effervescence, layered color, citrus twist.
Price range (PEN)Lean S/. 13 · Moderate S/. 15 · Growth S/. 18

Stop / revision ruleIf after 4 weeks sales are <4 units/day, kill or move to summer-seasonal only.

P-03 · Filtrado de la Casa (anchor filter, daily rotating)

Concept — A daily-rotating house filter coffee, always available, always at a fixed accessible price. Anchors the filter menu with a low-risk entry point for customers who hesitate at single-origin prices. Different from Filtrado Lonya (single origin, premium) — this is the everyday filter.

CriterionAssessment
Customer needCustomers want a quick, affordable, high-quality cup without choosing a single origin.
Brand fitHigh — codifies "exploramos diversos orígenes" (CoffeePass) as a daily promise.
Taste conceptBalanced, approachable, mild acidity, chocolate/nut-leaning blend.
Preparation timeBatch brew (60–90 minutes prep; 0 minutes per cup).
Ingredient overlapHigh — uses roastery's blend or rotating single origin.
TrainingLow — batch brew recipe card.
EquipmentBatch brewer (Curtis or similar). If not on hand, use large French press or Chemex batch.
WasteMedium — batch brew discards every 2 hours; sell-through must be calibrated.
Delivery suitabilityMedium — degrades in transit; better in-store.
Visual appealLow — humble ceramic cup. Visual appeal comes from the daily-origin chalkboard.
Price range (PEN)Lean S/. 8 · Moderate S/. 9 · Growth S/. 10
Margin potentialHigh — lowest cost-per-cup in the menu.
Operational complexityLow once dial-in is logged.
Test methodRun as morning-only (7:30–10:30) for 4 weeks.
Success metric≥15 cups/day; ≥10% of buyers add a pastry.
Stop / revision ruleIf after 4 weeks sales are <8 cups/day, kill batch brewer; if waste is >20%, shorten discard window to 90 min.

Competitor-informed pricing adjustments

A second research pass (see Module 02 §2.5) surfaced verified competitor prices that calibrate Ursa's pricing scenarios:

  • Retail beans (250g) · Puku Puku anchor is S/. 49. Ursa's previous S/. 28–48 was underpriced. New scenarios: S/. 32 / 42 / 52 for standard single-origins; S/. 42 / 50 / 58 for premium / Black Label.
  • Doble Barra Ursa bundle (P-01) · Neira's 2-person combo at S/. 55.20 (S/. 27.60/head) anchors a "Doble Barra Ursa Duo" variant at S/. 48 / 56 / 64 for two — same bundle mechanics, shared format.
  • Paid tasting (P-23) · Terrua anchors at US$25 (S/. 93). Ursa's Cata Privada at S/. 45/person is well below, with a stronger value stack (4 origins + paired pastries + take-home bag + roaster Q&A).
3.4

Pairing strategy — turning the menu into a story

Ursa already pairs drinks with food in verified Instagram posts (Ursagroni + empanada, Filtrado Lonya + financier). The plan formalizes this as a permanent menu section with three documented pairings, each priced below the sum of its parts. Pairings serve three jobs: they increase average ticket, they reduce ordering friction, and they tell the brand's two-bar story in a single tray.

Pairing 1 · La Ursa

Ursagroni + Empanada de carne con bechamel

Bitter-citrus-sweet cocktail meets rich savory pastry. Late-afternoon or evening occasion.

Pairing price · S/. 28 (lean) · S/. 32 (moderate) · S/. 36 (growth)

Pairing 2 · La Lonya

Filtrado Lonya, Utcubamba, Amazonas + Financier de pera

Single-origin Amazonas filter meets pear-almond pastry. Morning or mid-afternoon occasion.

Pairing price · S/. 22 (lean) · S/. 26 (moderate) · S/. 30 (growth)

Pairing 3 · La Maracumango

Maracumango Coldbrew + Cookie de Cacao

Tropical-fruit coldbrew meets single-origin cacao cookie. All-day, especially summer afternoon.

Pairing price · S/. 18 (lean) · S/. 21 (moderate) · S/. 24 (growth)

Pairing rules

  • Always exactly three pairings on the menu. Three is enough to express variety; more overwhelms.
  • One pairing must be hot + savory. One must be filter + sweet. One must be cold + sweet.
  • Each pairing has a name (La Ursa, La Lonya, La Maracumango) following the same portmanteau/origin convention as drinks.
  • Pairing price is always 12–18% below the sum of its parts.
  • One pairing rotates per quarter to keep the menu alive.
3.5

Skeptical revision pass

Skeptic 1

"You're proposing 22 new products for a small café. Isn't that operational suicide?"

No — because the proposals are sequenced, not simultaneous. The 90-day plan in §07 schedules roughly 4–5 live tests at any one time, each with a stop rule. Failed tests are killed within 14–30 days. The 22 proposals represent the test pool from which the permanent menu is drawn; the permanent menu itself grows by no more than 2–3 items per quarter.

Skeptic 2

"You invented prices without knowing Ursa's costs. Aren't those numbers fiction?"

Prices are scenario ranges, not point estimates. Each price has lean / moderate / growth variants. The owner picks the variant that matches actual costs and market positioning. The plan explicitly forbids inventing exact costs (Module 03 introduction). When the owner supplies actual costs, the ranges collapse to point prices.

Skeptic 3

"The 'Black Label' name implies a premium program you can't verify Ursa runs."

The reference is honest. Research surfaced a "Black Label" concept tied to a Cusco Peru Yuraq Yaku origin in a different café's Instagram. This dossier treats it as inspiration for a naming convention Ursa could adopt, not as evidence that Ursa currently runs a Black Label program. The proposal is explicitly a new product (P-04), not a verification of an existing one.
