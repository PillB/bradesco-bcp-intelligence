Module 08 · Subscription Economics & Cutoff Calculator · New in revision round 3

Ursa Mañana · S/. 20/month, unlimited coffee 7–10am. The math says yes.

A new product proposal grounded in marginal-cost economics: at Ursa's own-roastery cost per cup (~S/. 1.50) and a 60% side-attach rate, the subscription is profitable at almost any reasonable usage pattern. This module includes the full economic framework, a side-pushing strategy, risk analysis, and an interactive cutoff calculator the owner can use live to test any scenario.

8.1

The idea · Ursa Mañana subscription

The offer

S/. 20/month · unlimited coffee (espresso, americano, filter, coldbrew — no milk drinks) Monday–Friday, 7:00–10:00am · 1 cup per visit · in-store only (no Rappi) · cancel anytime.

Why this works for Ursa specifically

  • Own-roastery keeps marginal cost at the floor. Verified specialty cost-per-cup research (VirginCoffee.org, 2025): US$0.75–1.20. Ursa roasts its own, so per-cup bean cost is ~S/. 0.50–0.90, total marginal cost ~S/. 1.20–1.80. See Sources E-28.
  • Two-bar layout doubles the value. Subscribers can choose espresso bar OR coldbrew bar each visit — perceived value = 2× a single-bar subscription.
  • 7–10am window is Ursa's lowest-traffic time. The subscription fills empty seats, doesn't displace paying customers.
  • No milk drinks. Milk is the highest-marginal-cost category (S/. 0.50–1.00/cup). Restricting to espresso/americano/filter/coldbrew protects margin.
  • In-store only. No Rappi = no delivery commission (typically 25–30%) = the math holds. Also drives foot traffic, which drives side attach.
  • 1 cup per visit. Prevents abuse (someone coming once and ordering 4 americanos "to go"). Encourages daily visits instead, which is what the model needs.

Why this works for the customer

  • Break-even at ~13 cups/month = ~3 visits/week. A daily morning customer breaks even in week 2.
  • Fixed cost = budget certainty. A daily customer pays S/. 1.54/cup equivalent instead of S/. 8–12 menu price.
  • Habit reinforcement. Once subscribed, the marginal cost of a visit is zero — Ursa becomes the default morning stop.
  • Status. "I'm a Ursa Mañana member" is a small identity marker — fits the bear / neighborhood / craft identity.
  • Limited risk. Cancel anytime. No annual lock-in. Customer can test for one month.

The risk that matters most · cannibalization, not per-cup cost

The subscription is profitable per-cup at almost any usage. The real risk is converting existing paying customers who already buy S/. 8–12 coffee per visit into S/. 20/month subscribers. If 30 existing daily customers (each spending S/. 240/month) convert, monthly revenue drops by S/. 7,200 (30 × S/. 220 lost) and rises by S/. 600 (30 × S/. 20 subscription) + S/. 810 side margin uplift (30 × 13 visits × 0.6 × S/. 4.50 × 0.46 net of cannibalized side sales). Net first-year revenue impact: approximately −S/. 5,790/month. This is recoverable in months 4–12 as the subscription attracts new customers who weren't coming before — but it is a real short-term cost. The pilot in EXP-11 is designed to measure cannibalization directly.

8.2

Economic framework · the five variables that decide everything

The subscription's profitability is a function of five variables. Get them right and the model prints money; get one wrong and it bleeds. The calculator in §8.4 lets the owner test any combination.

Variable 1 · Marginal cost per cup

What it is. The cost Ursa pays in beans, water, cup, and milk (if applicable) for one additional cup of coffee. Not the menu price, not the average cost (which includes rent and labor). Pure variable cost.

Verified benchmark. Specialty coffee marginal cost is US$0.75–1.20 per cup (VirginCoffee.org, 2025). Ursa roasts its own, so the bean component is at the floor: Peru specialty green ≈ S/. 25–40/kg, roasted yield ~80%, so 18g espresso shot ≈ S/. 0.56–0.90 in beans. Plus cup/water S/. 0.30–0.50, plus milk for milk drinks S/. 0.50–1.00.

Default for calculator: S/. 1.50. This is the blended average of espresso, americano, filter, and coldbrew (no milk drinks allowed on subscription). Conservative — actual cost is likely S/. 1.20–1.40.

Ursa's own-roastery is the structural advantage that makes this subscription possible. A café buying roasted beans wholesale would face S/. 2.50–3.50/cup and the math breaks.

Variable 2 · Visits per week

What it is. How many times per week a subscriber actually shows up. Set the upper bound at 5 (one per weekday) because the subscription is Mon–Fri only.

Behavioral pattern. Subscription services see a "subscribed but inactive" tail: ~30% of subscribers use the service heavily (4–5 visits/week), ~50% moderately (2–3 visits/week), ~20% lightly (0–1 visits/week). The blended average settles around 2.5–3 visits/week.

Default for calculator: 3 visits/week. At 4.33 weeks/month that's 13 visits/month. This is realistic, not optimistic.

The 7–10am window caps abuse: even a power user can visit at most 5 times/week, and most won't.

Variable 3 · Side attach rate

What it is. The percentage of visits where the subscriber also buys a side — a cookie, financier, empanada, or retail bean bag. This is the variable that makes or breaks the model.

Why it matters. If subscribers only drink coffee (0% attach), the subscription is barely break-even. If subscribers attach at 60%, the subscription is wildly profitable. The whole strategic push is to drive attach rate up via in-store merchandising, paired recommendations, and the two-bar layout's natural upsell.

Default for calculator: 60%. Coffee-shop industry benchmarks for morning attach range 30–55%; Ursa's in-house food program and named-pairing convention (La Ursa, La Lonya, La Maracumango — see §3.4) justify a 60% assumption. The pilot in EXP-11 measures this directly.

The §8.3 side-pushing strategy is the lever that turns this variable from assumption to fact.

Variable 4 · Average side margin

What it is. The profit (not revenue) per attached side. Cookie at S/. 6 with S/. 1.50 cost = S/. 4.50 margin. Financier at S/. 8 with S/. 2.50 cost = S/. 5.50 margin. Empanada at S/. 12 with S/. 4 cost = S/. 8 margin.

Default for calculator: S/. 4.50. Conservative — the cookie is Ursa's most common side and lowest margin. If subscribers shift toward pastries or savory, margin rises.

In-house food production is what makes side margin high. Buy-in pastries would halve the margin and break the model.

Variable 5 · Cannibalization rate

What it is. The percentage of subscribers who would have bought coffee at full price anyway. A daily customer who used to pay S/. 10/visit × 13 visits/month = S/. 130/month, and now pays S/. 20/month, represents S/. 110 in cannibalized revenue (offset by S/. ~30 in retained side margin and operational savings).

Default for calculator: 70%. Most subscribers in a neighborhood café will be existing customers. The strategic value is the 30% who are new — drawn in by the subscription offer.

Cannibalization is the variable the owner can't directly control — but the pilot's design (EXP-11) measures it precisely by tracking pre-subscription vs post-subscription spend per customer.

Non-variables · fixed costs and labor

The subscription does not increase rent, equipment, or staffing during 7–10am (staff is already there; bar is already open). These are sunk costs and are excluded from the marginal analysis. Including them would double-count.

The subscription does not require new equipment, new SKUs, or new staff training beyond a 10-minute onboarding on the subscription sign-up flow and the side-pushing script.

If marginal cost calculations included fixed-cost allocation, every café subscription would look unprofitable. The right frame is marginal-cost economics.

8.3

Side-pushing strategy · how to drive the attach rate

The subscription's profitability is contingent on subscribers buying sides. The attach rate is not a fixed number — it is a variable the staff can directly influence. The strategy below lists every lever.

1 Welcome Subscriber shows app/card. Barista greets by name.
2 Offer "Tu café de la mañana. ¿Te lo acompañamos con algo hoy?" (scripted, every visit)
3 Pair Name the daily pairing: "Hoy: V60 Lonya + financier de pera, S/. 6"
4 Visible Pastry case at eye level on the path to the bar. Not behind the register.

How these numbers are computed

Net profit / subscriber / month (marginal) = subscription + side_margin − coffee_cost where:

  • visits_month = visits_week × weeks_month
  • cups_month = visits_month × cups_per_visit
  • coffee_cost = cups_month × cost_per_cup
  • side_margin = visits_month × (attach_rate / 100) × side_margin_per_attach

Free-cup capacity = (subscription + side_margin) / cost_per_cup — the number of cups a subscriber could drink before Ursa stops making money on them, given current attach rate.

Cutoff attach rate = (coffee_cost − subscription) / (visits_month × side_margin_per_attach) — the attach rate at which net profit hits zero. If negative, the subscription is profitable even at 0% attach.

Cannibalization-adjusted profit = net_profit_marginal − (cannibal_rate × (avg_spend_pre − subscription − retained_side_margin)) — accounts for revenue lost from customers who used to pay full price.

8.5

Risk analysis & safeguards

Risk 1 · Cannibalization of paying customers

Likelihood · High. Severity · Medium.

Most early subscribers will be existing customers. If 30 daily customers convert, short-term revenue drops ~S/. 5,790/month (see §8.1).

Safeguard · EXP-11 pilot measures cannibalization directly. Stop rule: if cannibalization-adjusted profit per subscriber is below S/. 0 for 60 days, kill the subscription.

Risk 2 · Side attach rate collapse

Likelihood · Medium. Severity · High.

If subscribers drink coffee and leave (0% attach), the subscription is barely break-even at default assumptions.

Safeguard · Side-pushing strategy in §8.3 is mandatory staff training, not optional. Track attach rate per subscriber weekly. If average attach falls below 35% for 2 consecutive weeks, escalate.

Risk 3 · Power-user abuse

Likelihood · Low. Severity · Medium.

A subscriber visiting 5x/week, 2 cups/visit, 0 sides = 43 cups/month = S/. 64.50 in coffee cost against S/. 20 in subscription revenue = S/. 44.50 loss/month.

Safeguard · 1 cup per visit enforced by staff. "No para llevar" on subscription cups. If a subscriber averages >4 visits/week for 3 consecutive weeks, send a friendly "we love you, here's a free cookie" message and watch attach rate.

Risk 4 · Brand-positioning dilution

Likelihood · Low. Severity · Medium.

"Unlimited coffee for S/. 20" can read as commodity, not specialty. Could undermine the premium positioning Ursa's named-portmanteau drinks and single-origin program build.

Safeguard · Frame as "Ursa Mañana", not "unlimited coffee". Position as a neighborhood ritual, not a discount. Subscribers get the same single origins, same craft, same bar — they just pay differently. Never use the word "ilimitado" in marketing.

Stop rules summary (EXP-11)

  • Kill if · cannibalization-adjusted profit per subscriber < S/. 0 at day 60.
  • Iterate if · 30 < attach rate < 45% at day 30. Redesign side-pushing script and pairing chalkboard; retest 30 days.
  • Scale if · attach rate ≥ 50% AND cannibalization-adjusted profit ≥ S/. 8/subscriber/month at day 60. Open subscription to all customers; remove pilot cap.
  • Hard ceiling · 100 active subscribers in pilot. Beyond this, the 7–10am window gets crowded and paying customers are displaced.
8.6

Pilot design · EXP-11

90-day capped pilot. 100-subscriber maximum. Direct measurement of every variable in the economic model. No marketing spend beyond Instagram Stories and a table card — the offer must pull on its own merits.

Why this pilot is safe

  • Capped at 100 subscribers — even in the worst case (0% attach, 5 visits/week, 2 cups/visit), maximum monthly loss = 100 × S/. 64.50 = S/. 6,450. Bounded and survivable.
  • 90-day window — short enough to kill cleanly if it fails; long enough to see habit formation in cohorts.
  • Direct measurement — every variable in the economic model is measured, not estimated. The calculator's assumptions become facts.
  • No paid media — the offer has to pull on its own. If 100 subscribers don't sign up organically in 30 days, the offer isn't compelling enough to scale.
  • Grandfather clause — early subscribers keep S/. 20 for 6 months even if price rises. Builds loyalty; prevents "they raised the price" churn.
8.7

Skeptical revision pass

Skeptic 1

"This is the classic café mistake. Subscription coffee never works."

The classic failure mode is buying roasted beans wholesale and offering unlimited milk drinks all day. Both destroy margin. This proposal (a) uses Ursa's own-roastery marginal cost floor, (b) restricts to non-milk drinks, (c) caps at 7–10am weekdays, (d) enforces 1 cup per visit, (e) excludes delivery. Each restriction exists to neutralize a specific failure mode. The calculator in §8.4 makes the math transparent — anyone can test their own assumptions.

Skeptic 2

"You're assuming 60% attach rate. That's optimistic."

Fair. The calculator defaults to 60%, but the sensitivity table in §8.4 shows profit at every attach rate from 0% to 100%. At 0% attach and 3 visits/week, profit is S/. 0.50/subscriber/month — basically break-even. At 30% attach (industry-low end), profit is S/. 17.80. The model degrades gracefully. The pilot in EXP-11 directly measures actual attach rate, so the assumption becomes a fact within 30 days.

Skeptic 3

"Cannibalization will eat all the profit."

Partially true in month 1–3, which is why the calculator includes a cannibalization-adjusted profit line. The default assumes 70% cannibalization at S/. 130/month lost spend — and the model still recovers within months 4–12 as new (non-cannibalizing) subscribers join. The pilot's stop rule explicitly kills the program if cannibalization-adjusted profit is negative at day 60. The owner loses nothing by testing.
