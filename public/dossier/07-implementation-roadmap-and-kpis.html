Module 07 · Implementation Roadmap & KPIs

A plan that names who does what, by when, with what metric, and when to stop.

This module converts every recommendation in the dossier into a sequenced, owned, measured plan: first 72 hours, 30 days, 60 days, 90 days, and a 12-month innovation roadmap. Each workstream names its owner, dependency, metric, and stop rule. Three budget scenarios in PEN. Includes the skeptical-revision log and the final spirit-preservation verdict required by the completion standard.

7.1

First 72 hours — the launch sequence

The first 72 hours establish the foundation. Everything in this window costs S/. 0–200 in cash and depends only on owner + staff time. Nothing here is irreversible.

Hour 0–4 · Day 1 morning

Audit & align

Owner reads Modules 01–04 in full. Confirms or corrects the verified identity markers in §1.1. Answers the six open questions in Sources & Evidence if possible. Identifies the budget scenario (lean / moderate / growth) for the first 30 days.

Owner · Owner · Dependency · None · Output · Budget scenario selected; open questions answered or deferred

Set up WhatsApp Business (free). Print Prototype 6.7's service-recovery card variant: "Si algo no estuvo bien, cuéntanos aquí" with WhatsApp link. Place on every bill. Train staff to ask for Google reviews from happy regulars (one ask per visit, max).

Owner · Owner + store manager · Dependency · Phone with WhatsApp · Cost · S/. 40 · Metric · WhatsApp live; cards in bills; ≥2 review asks on Day 3

Day 60 decision gate

At day 60, the team should have 8 weeks of data. Three questions: (1) Which acquisition channel produced the most attributed visits per S/. spent? Double it. (2) Which retention mechanism produced the highest return rate? Make it permanent. (3) Which experiment failed? Kill cleanly and document in experiments.json. Do not extend failed experiments out of sunk-cost loyalty.

7.5

12-month innovation roadmap

Beyond 90 days, the roadmap sequences four quarter-sized moves. Each move is a hypothesis tested against the same metric structure used in the 90-day plan. None of these moves are committed; they are scenarios for the owner to choose from based on 90-day results.

Months 4–6 · Quarter 2

Q2 · Tourism & hospitality pipeline

If EXP-05 (hotel sampling) succeeded, formalize a Miraflores tourism pipeline: 15+ hotels stocked with Ursa guides, 2 Airbnb Experience hosts including Ursa, 1 producer visit hosted in store. Test a "Ursa in your room" minibar kit with 1 boutique hotel. Goal: tourism-attributed visits = 15% of total monthly traffic.

Owner · Owner · Dependency · EXP-05 success · Metric · 15% tourism-attributed visits · Stop · If <8% by month 6, defer tourism to Q4 and pivot to B2B

Months 7–9 · Quarter 3

Q3 · B2B & wholesale

If EXP-08 (office subscription) succeeded, scale to 10+ active office subscriptions and explore wholesale to 2–3 Miraflores restaurants for their post-meal coffee. Test a "Ursa at the office" weekly cupping add-on for subscription customers. Goal: B2B revenue = 20% of monthly total.

Owner · Owner + B2B lead · Dependency · EXP-08 success · Metric · 20% B2B revenue · Stop · If <10% by month 9, defer wholesale and refocus on retail

Months 10–12 · Quarter 4

Q4 · Anniversary & second location hypothesis

Use the Ursa anniversary (or end-of-year) to publish the "One year of grams" montage (Script 10) and a public thank-you. 7.6

Owners, dependencies & stopping rules — summary

Every workstream in the 90-day plan names an owner (who is accountable), a dependency (what must be true to start), a metric (how success is measured), and a stop rule (when to kill or revise). The summary below is the cheat-sheet version.

Owners (5 distinct roles)

  • Owner — strategic decisions, partnerships, budget, B2B, asset pack sourcing.
  • Store manager — in-store operations, Rappi, first-visit bundle execution, service recovery.
  • Head barista — cupping program, dial-in discipline, barista training, in-store craft visibility.
  • Roaster — bean sourcing, roast profiles, retail bean line, bean info cards.
  • Social lead — content production, creator collaborations, WhatsApp list, UGC mechanisms, weekly metrics review.

For a small café, one person may hold two roles (e.g., Owner + Store manager). The plan does not assume five distinct people.

Stopping rules — universal principles

  • Kill cleanly. A failed experiment is killed within 14–30 days of its stop rule triggering. No extension out of sunk-cost loyalty.
  • Document the failure. Every kill is logged in experiments.json with the reason. Future experiments do not repeat the same hypothesis in the same form.
  • Iterate once. If a workstream is partially working (50–80% of target), redesign one variable and retest once. If the second iteration also fails, kill.
  • Never double down on failure. Paid amplification never rescues underperforming organic content (per §5.7).
  • Escalate to owner. If a workstream is critical to the 90-day success criteria and is failing, escalate to the owner within 7 days of detection.

7.8

Skeptical-revision log (3 rounds)

The completion standard requires up to three skeptical revision rounds. The log below records what each skeptic challenged and how the plan responded. This is the final recorded revision pass; the dossier is now stable.

Round 1 · Is the plan too aggressive for a small café?

Challenge · "Ursa is a single-location café with limited staff. The 90-day plan and 12-month roadmap assume an operational capacity that may not exist."

Response · The plan was re-checked against capacity. The 72-hour plan costs S/. 120–200 and 8–12 hours of owner time. The 30-day plan can be run by an owner + 1 social lead + existing staff. The 60- and 90-day plans explicitly defer Level-2 design rollout and B2B scaling if the 30-day decision gate is missed. The roadmap degrades gracefully: if any workstream fails, the plan continues with the remaining workstreams.

Outcome: plan stands, with explicit degradation paths added at each decision gate.

Round 2 · Is the spirit of Ursa actually preserved?

Challenge · "The plan adds companion marks, paired menus, and seasonal expressions. Doesn't this dilute the existing brand?"

Response · Every addition was checked against the §1.1 verified identity markers. Companion marks (paw, silhouette, constellation, profile line) extend the bear identity without touching the official logo. The two-bar paired menu operationalizes the verified two-bar concept. Level-3 seasonal expressions are reversible and limited to 4–6 weeks. The official logo is treated as immutable throughout the dossier. The conservative-refinement direction (Level 1 first, Level 2 from month 4, Level 3 seasonal only) ensures nothing is replaced — only refined.

Outcome: plan stands, with §1.4 bear-grammar rules and §1.9 evolution-level rules tightened to forbid any addition that dilutes the official mark.

Round 3 · Are the metrics realistic?

Challenge · "Targets like +25% direction requests, ≥30% return rate, and 15% tourism-attributed visits are guesses. How do we know they're achievable?"

Response · We don't — and the plan says so. Every metric is paired with a stop rule that triggers if the target is missed by >50%. The 30-day decision gate explicitly downgrades the budget scenario if 4 or fewer of 12 KPIs hit target. The metrics are calibrated to be ambitious enough to motivate but not so ambitious that they encourage gaming. If actual performance is consistently below target, the targets themselves are revised at the quarterly review.

Outcome: plan stands, with explicit "targets are hypotheses, not promises" language added to the decision gates.

7.9

Final spirit-preservation verdict

The completion standard requires a final spirit-preservation verdict. The verdict below is the dossier's final word on whether the plan preserves Ursa's existing identity.

Verdict · Spirit preserved.

The plan documented in this dossier preserves and refines Ursa Coffee Roasters' verified identity — Art Nouveau lean, browns-and-greens palette, bear motif, in-house roastery, two-bar architecture, "Un gramo a la vez" tagline, named-portmanteau drinks, in-house food program, and warm-expert voice. No element of the verified identity is removed. No element of the official logo is altered, parodied, or counterfeited.

The plan adds structure (codified palette, typography, voice, photography direction, bear grammar), operationalizes existing assets (two-bar concept, tagline, pairing convention, named-drink convention), and tests new product and channel hypotheses through low-cost experiments with explicit stop rules. Every addition is reversible; every experiment can be killed cleanly; every budget scenario degrades gracefully.

The recommended direction is conservative refinement (Level 1) as the permanent system, distinctive growth (Level 2) as a skin from month 4, and experimental seasonal expression (Level 3) only for limited periods. Total rebrand is explicitly off the table. The bear, the gram, and the green are protected throughout.
