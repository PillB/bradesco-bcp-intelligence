Module 01 · Brand Audit & Design System

Verify what is already true, then refine it carefully.

This module audits Ursa Coffee Roasters' current brand against owner-stated identity markers (Art Nouveau, browns and greens, bear motif, specialty roasting, warm/crafted atmosphere), codifies a conservative-refinement design system, and proposes three evolution levels with a single recommendation. The official logo is never altered or counterfeited; the geometric bear glyph used in the dossier header is an original concept mark for navigation only.

Verified inputs · Instagram, Facebook, Rappi, CoffeePass, TripAdvisor Snapshot · 2026-08-01 Recommendation · Level 1 permanent · Level 2 growth skin · Level 3 seasonal only
1.1

The owner brief lists five identity markers: Art Nouveau visual language, browns and greens palette, bear motif or mascot, specialty-coffee and roasting expertise, and a warm/artistic/crafted atmosphere. The table below records how each marker appears in current public materials. Each finding is tagged verified, partial, unverified, or gap.

What is already consistent

  • Roastery-first positioning across Instagram bio, CoffeePass, and product naming.
  • Single-origin transparency on at least one verified beverage (Filtrado Lonya, Utcubamba, Amazonas).
  • Tagline applied consistently in the Instagram bio.
  • In-house food program (pastry and savory) positioned as house-made, not bought-in.
  • Two-bar concept used as a promotional lever.

What is inconsistent or under-leveraged

  • Review channels are empty. TripAdvisor listing exists with zero reviews. Google Business Profile presence and photo quality are unverified and likely thin.
  • Tagline is bio-only. "Un gramo a la vez" does not appear to be systematized across content, packaging, or in-store signage in any verifiable way.
  • Two-bar concept is promotion-only. It surfaces in a discount post, not as a permanent identity asset.
  • Bear identity has no public-facing grammar. Without rules, the bear risks being inconsistent across packaging, social, and merch.
  • Art Nouveau lean is asserted but not codified. Without codification, future designers and creators will diverge.

What is distinctive (protect these)

  • The brand name Ursa + the bear = a memorable, ownable character in a category where most competitors are named after founders or streets.
  • "Un gramo a la vez" = a precision-and-patience story that almost no competitor tells.
  • Two-bar (espresso + coldbrew) layout = an operational distinction that doubles as a visual and naming system.
  • Named, portmanteau-style drink names (Ursagroni, Maracumango) = a naming convention that signals craft and playfulness at the same time.

What is missing (close these in 30 days)

  • No documented photography style. Without one, Instagram will drift toward generic coffee-stock aesthetics.
  • No documented caption language rules. Spanish/English/emoji mix is unmanaged.
  • No documented call-to-action library. CTAs are ad hoc.
  • No reusable social template system. Every post is bespoke, which slows output and erodes consistency.
  • No event or class format documented. Customer-education value is latent.
  • No partnership or B2B asset. Hotels, hostels, and offices have nothing to receive.

1.2

Design principles

Five principles govern every visual and verbal decision in this system. They are written so any future designer, barista, or content creator can apply them without re-reading the rest of the dossier.

01 · Roast first, decor second

Every layout leads with the coffee. Roast date, origin, and preparation method appear before decoration. Art Nouveau flourishes frame information; they never crowd it out.

02 · Field to cup, brown to green

The palette is not arbitrary; it is the brand's story. Browns move from green-bean to espresso along the roast curve. Greens move from forest-deep to leaf along the canopy curve. Use brown when the message is about coffee; use green when the message is about origin, atmosphere, or community.

03 · The bear is a character, not a logo

The official bear mark stays where it is (logo lockups). Beyond that, the bear can appear as silhouette, paw, constellation (Ursa Major), or implied through posture in illustration. It is never cartoonish, never a generic stock bear, and never placed where it competes with the official logo.

04 · One gram at a time

Precision is visible. Grams, ratios, temperatures, and times appear in copy when relevant. Layouts leave breathing room — never crowd. The visual equivalent of "one gram at a time" is generous margins and a single, decisive focal point per surface.

05 · Two bars, paired motifs

The two-bar layout is reflected in the design system: paired columns, mirrored motifs, dual labels (barra espresso / barra coldbrew). This is the system's structural signature and the most ownable visual asset beyond the bear.

1.3

Color palette

Two structural ramps — Roast Spectrum (browns) and Andean Forest (greens) — sit on a paper-and-milk neutral base and are accented by two Art Nouveau colors: gilded gold and terracotta. This is a conservative refinement: nothing in the owner-stated palette is removed, and the ramps give every brown and green a clear job.

Usage rules

  • Body text always uses Espresso (#211208) on Paper (#FAF5EC) or Cream (#F4EBD9). Never on accent colors.
  • Headings use Dark Roast (#3B2417). Forest Deep (#2D4A36) is allowed for environmental and origin-themed headings only.
  • Gold is reserved for: rules, hairlines, seals, foil-stamp-style labels, and a single accent per layout. Never as a fill behind text.
  • Terracotta is reserved for: seasonal and limited-edition signals, heat/temperature cues, and the coldbrew bar's "warm rim" accent (it visually opposes the green of the espresso bar).
  • Photography backgrounds default to Paper or Cream. Avoid pure white (#FFFFFF) — it reads as pharmaceutical, not crafted.
  • Accessibility: every text/background pairing in this system meets WCAG AA contrast (4.5:1) when used as specified. Gold-on-cream combinations are decorative only and never carry body text.
1.4

Bear motif & companion marks

The official Ursa bear mark (logo) is treated as immutable in this dossier. What this section codifies is the grammar around it: when to use the logo, when to use companion marks, and what postures and contexts are allowed.

Official bear mark — usage rules

  • Primary surface · storefront, menu cover, primary social avatar, retail bean bag front, official invoice.
  • Clear space · at least the height of the bear's ear around the mark on all sides.
  • Minimum size · 24px on screen, 8mm in print. Below these sizes, switch to a companion mark.
  • Forbidden · do not recolor, do not stretch, do not rotate, do not add drop shadows, do not place on photographs without a protective panel, do not combine with another logo.

Companion marks — for non-logo contexts

These are original marks designed to extend the bear's world without touching the official logo. They are used as decorative elements, watermarks, story stickers, and packaging detail.

Paw Silhouette Constellation Profile line

Bear postures — what is and is not allowed

  • Allowed · standing (vigilance), seated (welcome), walking (journey), reaching up (curiosity, "exploring" per CoffeePass description).
  • Forbidden · aggressive postures (roaring, claws raised), cartoonish expressions, drinking coffee from a mug (too cliché), wearing clothing or accessories, holding branded objects.
  • Seasonal exception · the constellation mark may be inverted (winter sky) for the June–August cold-and-cocoa season only, paired with terracotta accents.
1.5

Typography

Three typefaces form the system. All three are available as free-to-use web fonts (Google Fonts) and have desktop equivalents for print production. Fallback stacks ensure the system works even without internet.

Cormorant Garamond

Display & headings

A high-contrast serif with a subtle Art Nouveau curve in its terminals. Used for: H1–H3, menu item names, bean names, poster headlines, packaging display lines.

Fallback: Georgia, Times New Roman. Weights: 400, 500, 600, 700 + italic.

Inter

Body & UI

A humanist sans with excellent on-screen legibility. Used for: body copy, captions, ingredient lists, table data, navigation, app and email interfaces.

Fallback: system-ui, -apple-system, Helvetica Neue, Arial. Weights: 300, 400, 500, 600, 700.

Oswald

Labels & eyebrows

A condensed sans with letter-spaced caps. Used for: section eyebrows, table headers, price tags, category labels, "Un gramo a la vez" lockups.

Fallback: Inter, system-ui. Weights: 300, 400, 500, 600.

1.6

Art Nouveau components

Art Nouveau is the system's structural ornament. It is used as framing — never as decoration for its own sake. The components below are built as SVG primitives so they can be reused in any surface without raster images.

Whiplash line

Used as section dividers and frame tops. One stroke weight (1.4px), one accent dot color (Gold).

Botanical bracket

Framing for menu headers, certificates, and event materials. Botanical forms echo the forest palette.

Gold seal

Foil-stamp-style seal for packaging labels, certificates, and limited editions. Always Gold-on-Cream.

Layout grammar — Art Nouveau applied

  • Asymmetric balance · off-center focal points with botanical brackets restoring balance on the opposite side.
  • Vertical rhythm · wide vertical margins (≥40px on screen, ≥16mm in print) — the visual equivalent of "one gram at a time."
  • Single decisive flourish per surface · never more than one whiplash line or botanical bracket per layout. The flourish frames information; it does not compete with it.
  • Pairing the two bars · whenever a layout shows both bars, use mirrored brackets or paired columns to express the duality.
  • Forbidden · drop shadows on type, gradients behind text, more than two accent colors in one layout, ALL CAPS body copy, decorative fonts, skeuomorphic wood/coffee-bean textures.
  • Forbidden · Art Nouveau used as background pattern behind body text. Nouveau is a frame, not a wallpaper.
  • Forbidden · mixing the Cormorant Garamond italic with a different display italic in the same layout.
1.7

Voice & caption language

Ursa's voice is warm expert: knowledgeable without condescension, crafted without preciousness, Peruvian without cliché. The table below codifies the voice across four registers so any writer (staff, creator, agency) can apply it consistently.

Table 1.7 — Voice registers
RegisterWhen to useExample (Spanish, Peru)Do not
Roaster Bean cards, single-origin descriptions, brew-method notes, cupping announcements. "Lonya, Utcubamba, Amazonas. 1,750 m. Lavado. Tueste medio-alto. A copa: azúcar panela, manzana verde, final de cacao. Un gramo a la vez." Generic flavor wheels, "cuerpo medio, acidez media".
Host Daily social posts, Stories, in-store signage, welcome messages. "Hoy abrimos a las 7:30. Te esperamos en Alcanfores 183 con un filtrado recién hecho y un financier de pera saliendo del horno." Corporate slogans, "¡Te esperamos!" alone without specificity.
Storyteller Reels and TikTok scripts, brand films, origin-trip content, blog/newsletter. "Tres gramos. Esa fue la diferencia entre el tueste de ayer y el de hoy. Esto es cómo lo decidimos." Over-narration, false profundity, English buzzwords ("craft", "elevated", "curated").
Guide Educational content, classes, cuppings, brewing guides, FAQ. "Si tu V60 sabe amargo y el agua no estaba tan caliente, baja la temperatura a 90°C y muele un poco más grueso. Te mostramos cómo." Snobbery, "depende" without a clear answer, jargon without explanation.

Caption language rules

  • Primary language · Spanish as spoken in Lima, Peru. Use "tú" not "usted" unless addressing an institution. Avoid peninsular Spanish ("vosotros", "ordenador").
  • English · allowed for international-tourism-facing content and hashtags only. Never mix English and Spanish inside a single sentence.
  • Emoji · maximum two per caption. Allowed: ☕ 🐻 🌿 🍑 🥭. Forbidden: 🤤 🔥 💯 🙌 and any "hype" emoji that breaks the warm-expert voice.
  • Hashtags · maximum five per post. Always include #SpecialtyCoffee. Rotate from a documented library in §06.
  • Numbers · grams, ratios, altitudes, temperatures, and times are always explicit. Never "the right amount" — always "18g" or "1:16".

Call-to-action library

A documented library avoids ad-hoc CTAs. Default CTAs:

  • "Te esperamos en Alcanfores 183." (default in-store)
  • "Pídelo por Rappi." (delivery)
  • "Cuéntanos en barra." (in-person conversation)
  • "Escríbenos por WhatsApp para reservar tu cupo." (events)
  • "Llévate el grano. Tuesta el lunes, llega el martes." (retail beans)
  • "Síguenos para la próxima fecha de cata." (cuppings)
1.8

Photography direction

Without a documented photography style, Ursa's Instagram will drift toward generic specialty-coffee aesthetics. This section gives any photographer (staff, contracted, or UGC creator) a brief that produces consistent results.

Subject priorities (in order)

  1. Hands and tools — portafilters, V60 drippers, scales, kettles, bean scoops. Hands always visible; faces optional.
  2. The pour and the drip — espresso extraction, V60 pour, coldbrew tap. Motion captured at 1/250s or faster.
  3. Food and drink on the bar — pastry, financier, empanada, coldbrew in glass. Never on white; always on Paper or Cream surface.
  4. Interior detail — the bear mark where it appears, the two bars, corner details, menu close-ups. Wide-angle interior shots are secondary.
  5. People — baristas at work, customers in conversation. Always candid, never posed for the camera. Hands and profile over faces.

Technical direction

  • Light · natural window light preferred. Warm Kelvin (3200–4000K) for interior evening; cool morning light (5000–5500K) acceptable for opening shots.
  • Background · Paper (#FAF5EC) or Cream (#F4EBD9). Real wood surfaces acceptable if not glossy. Avoid pure white backdrops.
  • Color treatment · warm shadows, neutral midtones, no oversaturated greens. No filters that turn browns orange or greens neon.
  • Composition · off-center, asymmetric, with negative space for type. Square (1:1) and 4:5 for posts; 9:16 for Reels and Stories.
  • Watermark · small bear silhouette in the lower-right corner, 4% opacity. Never on the subject.

What photography must not do

  • Top-down "flat lay" with seven props and a hand pouring — overused and undifferentiated.
  • Stock-style close-ups of a single coffee bean — meaningless without context.
  • Smoke or steam added in post — looks fake and breaks the warm-expert voice.
  • Customers' faces without consent — including in the background of interior shots.
  • Anything that could be confused with Punto Café, Neira, or any other competitor's feed at thumbnail size.
1.9

Three evolution levels & recommendation

The system offers three evolution levels. They are not three competing directions; they are three depths of change. Level 1 is the recommended permanent system. Level 2 is a growth skin layered on top of Level 1 from month 4 onward. Level 3 is a seasonal expression reserved for limited periods.

Recommended permanent

Level 1 · Conservative refinement

What changes: nothing visible to a customer who already knows Ursa. What changes internally: palette, typography, voice, and bear grammar are codified so every future touchpoint is consistent.

  • Palette codified as Roast Spectrum + Andean Forest + neutrals + accents.
  • Typography fixed at Cormorant Garamond + Inter + Oswald.
  • Tagline systematized across bio, packaging, signage, and content end-cards.
  • Two-bar concept promoted from promotion to permanent identity asset.
  • Photography brief documented and shared with all creators.

Risk · very low. Effort · 14 days. Cost · S/. 800–1,400 (printed signage + photo reshoot day + template build).

Growth skin from month 4

Level 2 · Distinctive growth system

What changes: Ursa becomes unmistakably itself at thumbnail size. The bear companion marks (paw, silhouette, constellation, profile line) appear across packaging, social templates, and merch. The two-bar paired-motif becomes the visual signature.

  • Companion marks rolled out across all non-logo surfaces.
  • Bean bag label redesigned as Level-2 system (see §06 prototype).
  • Paired-column menu and "barra espresso / barra coldbrew" labels in store.
  • Reusable social template library (carousel, Story, Reel cover) — see §06.
  • Merch line: bear-paw tamp mat, constellation mug, paired-bar tote.

Risk · low if Level 1 is in place. Effort · 6 weeks. Cost · S/. 3,800–6,200 (design + prototyping + first merch run + print).

Seasonal expression only

Level 3 · Experimental seasonal

What changes: for 4–6 week seasons (June–August cold-and-cocoa; December–January summer coldbrew; founder's-week anniversary), Ursa adopts a limited expression that pushes the system further — but always reversibly.

  • Constellation mark inverted (winter sky) for cold season.
  • Terracotta accent promoted to primary for summer coldbrew season.
  • Seasonal menu card with experimental typography (Cormorant italic large).
  • Limited-edition bean bag with seasonal seal (numbered, dated).
  • One seasonal beverage with portmanteau name following the Ursagroni / Maracumango convention.

Risk · medium if rules are ignored. Effort · 2 weeks per season. Cost · S/. 1,200–2,200 per season. Rule · never two Level-3 seasons active simultaneously.

Recommendation

Adopt Level 1 as the permanent design system in the first 30 days. Layer Level 2 as a growth skin from month 4 (after the Level-1 system is proven in daily use). Use Level 3 only as a seasonal expression, starting with the next available season after Level 2 launch. Do not skip Level 1 in pursuit of Level 2 — skipping is the single most common reason rebrands fail.

1.10

Brand audit log — skeptical revision pass

Three skeptical questions were asked of this module before publication. The answers are recorded so the owner can see the reasoning.

Skeptic 1

"You have not seen the official logo or packaging. How can you propose a design system?"

The system is built on what is verifiable (positioning, tagline, named products, two-bar layout, in-house food) plus owner-stated identity. It codifies principles, not pixels. The official bear mark is treated as immutable; companion marks are additive only. The owner-supplied asset pack (open question Q6) is required before any Level-2 production work begins.

Skeptic 2

"Cormorant Garamond + Inter + Oswald is a generic组合. Why these?"

Cormorant's high-contrast serif terminals carry the Art Nouveau curve without resorting to literal Art Nouveau display faces (which age badly and read as costume). Inter is the best-legibility humanist sans at small sizes for digital. Oswald's letter-spaced caps provide the editorial-label register without the overuse of Bebas Neue. All three are free, have Latin-script coverage, and have on-screen and print-grade weights.

Skeptic 3

"Aren't you turning a small café into a brand system it can't operate?"

The system is calibrated to a small operation. Level 1 requires no new staff and uses mostly in-house production. Templates in §06 are designed to be re-used, not bespoke per post. The experiments in experiments.json cost S/. 0–560 each. If the owner cannot afford Level 2 in month 4, the plan degrades gracefully to "Level 1 + selected Level 3 seasonal expressions" with no loss of identity.
