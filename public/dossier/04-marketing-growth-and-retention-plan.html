Module 04 · Marketing, Growth & Retention Plan

Make Ursa easy to encounter, easy to choose, easy to return to.

This module integrates positioning, jobs-to-be-done, offer architecture, customer journey, acquisition, retention, partnerships, tourism, hospitality, B2B, loyalty, referrals, reviews, service recovery, and measurement into one plan. It adapts Alex Hormozi's Value Equation and offer-stack discipline, and Rory Sutherland's perceived-value lens — without copying either. Three budget scenarios in PEN: lean (S/. 2,800/mo), moderate (S/. 7,200/mo), growth (S/. 16,500/mo).

4.1

Positioning statement

Para el bebedor de Miraflores que quiere café de especialidad sin la solemnidad de un laboratorio, Ursa Coffee Roasters es la rosticería de barrio donde el oso, el gramo y la barra doble (espresso + coldbrew) convierten cada visita en una pequeña ceremonia. No somos la cafetería más grande, ni la más cara, ni la más premiada. Somos la más recognizable — un gramo a la vez.

The statement above is the single source of truth for positioning. Every campaign, post, partnership, and offer in this module either reinforces it or is rejected. The statement is written in the warm-expert voice codified in §1.7, leads with the customer and the neighborhood (not with the brand), names the two-bar distinction operationally, and closes with the verified tagline.

What the positioning does not claim

  • Not "the best coffee in Lima" — unverifiable and generic.
  • Not "world-class" — borrowed authority Ursa has not earned publicly.
  • Not " specialty coffee pioneer" — Bisetti owns that claim.
  • Not "champion barista" — Neira owns that claim.
  • Not "cozy" — Puku Puku owns that claim in customer language.

What Ursa does claim: recognizable, neighborhood, two-bar, roastery-first, warm-expert. These are ownable and verified.

4.2

Target customers & jobs to be done

Four customer personas own the majority of Ursa's occasions. Each has a job-to-be-done (JTBD) framed in the customer's words. Offers, content, and partnerships are mapped to these personas; nothing is built for an undefined "everyone".

Persona 1

El vecino de Miraflores (the neighborhood regular)

Who · Lives or works within 10 blocks of Alcanfores 183. 28–55. Coffee is a daily ritual, not a hobby.

JTBD · "Ayúdame a empezar el día con algo rico sin tener que pensarlo mucho." (Help me start the day with something good without thinking too much.)

Lever · Speed at the bar, daily filter, recognizable staff, loyalty that rewards repeat visits.

Persona 2

La curiosa de especialidad (the specialty curious)

Who · 24–40. Has heard of specialty coffee, wants to learn, hesitant to ask "dumb" questions.

JTBD · "Enséñame qué es el café de especialidad sin hacerme sentir ignorante." (Teach me what specialty coffee is without making me feel ignorant.)

Lever · First-visit bundle (P-01), Saturday cupping (P-08), guide-style content, guide-on-duty staff behavior.

Persona 3

El turista de café (the coffee tourist)

Who · Domestic or international visitor staying in a Miraflores hotel or hostel. Wants a memorable cup and a story to take home.

JTBD · "Llévame a un café que sea claramente peruano y claramente bueno." (Take me to a café that is clearly Peruvian and clearly good.)

Lever · Hotel concierge sampling (EXP-05), Google Business Profile (EXP-07), tourism partnerships, single-origin bean bag as souvenir.

Persona 4

El office manager (the B2B buyer)

Who · Office manager or founder at a Miraflores coworking, agency, or studio. Wants good coffee for the team without operational complexity.

JTBD · "Dame café bueno para la oficina sin tener que pensar en abastecimiento." (Give me good coffee for the office without thinking about supply.)

Lever · Office subscription pilot (EXP-08), 1kg bean delivery, filter loan, weekly check-in.

4.3

Message architecture

Three layers: a single brand promise, three proof pillars, and a library of supporting messages. Every external communication maps to one pillar; nothing is "off-brand".

Brand promise (always)

Un gramo a la vez. Café de especialidad, tostado en Miraflores.

Pillar 1 · Roastery-first

Proof: in-house roasting, named single origins (Lonya, Utcubamba, Amazonas), bean cards with origin/altitude/process, roast date on every bag.

Pillar 2 · Two-bar distinction

Proof: barra espresso + barra coldbrew; paired menus; named-portmanteau drinks (Ursagroni, Maracumango, Durazno Clarificado).

Pillar 3 · Bear, neighborhood, craft

Proof: bear motif, Alcanfores 183 address, in-house pastry (financier, empanada, cookies), warm-expert service.

Supporting message library (used as needed)

  • Acquisition · "Si es tu primera vez, empieza por la Doble Barra."
  • Education · "Sábado 11am. Cata gratuita en barra. Cupos limitados."
  • Retail · "Llévate el grano. Tuesta el lunes, llega el martes."
  • Delivery · "Pídelo por Rappi. Mismo café, misma casa."
  • Events · "Catas privadas para 4–8. Escríbenos por WhatsApp."
  • Tourism · "Café peruano, tostado en Miraflores. Llévate el origen."
  • B2B · "Café para tu oficina. Suscripción semanal, sin complicaciones."
  • Service recovery · "Si algo no estuvo bien, cuéntanos aquí. Lo arreglamos."

4.4

Offer architecture (adapted from Hormozi, not copied)

Hormozi's Value Equation is Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort and Sacrifice). For a café, the equation translates cleanly: dream outcome = the memorable cup; likelihood = visible craft and named origin; time delay = queue + brew time; effort = decision friction + payment. The offers below are designed to maximize the numerator and minimize the denominator. None of the offer wording is copied from Hormozi's materials.

Acquisition offer · Doble Barra Ursa

Stack · 1 espresso-bar drink + 1 coldbrew-bar drink + 1 house pastry + a "first-visit" bean sample (10g) + a 1-page "Si es tu primera vez" guide.

Anchor · S/. 34 (sum of parts, normal price). Bundle price · S/. 26 (moderate scenario).

Why it works · Expresses two-bar identity in one transaction; reduces first-visit decision friction; provides a small retail sample (Hormozi's "give before you ask"); 24% discount is meaningful but not destructive.

See EXP-01 for test design and stop rule.

Continuity offer · Bean Drop WhatsApp list

Stack · 24-hour early access to new single origins + first-right-of-refusal on limited bags + a free Saturday cupping seat reserved for list members + occasional "roaster's pick" recommendation.

Anchor · Standard retail bean price. List price · Free to join; beans at normal price.

Why it works · Creates scarcity and rewards loyalty without discounting. Direct channel that bypasses Instagram algorithm. Continuity is behavioral, not contractual — but very effective.

See EXP-06 for test design and stop rule.

Retention offer · Cupping + 10% retail

Stack · Free Saturday cupping (20 min, capped 6) + 10% off any retail bean purchased same-day + a follow-up WhatsApp with the cupping notes.

Anchor · S/. 0 (free cupping) + S/. 28–48 bean. Effective price · S/. 25–43 after discount.

Why it works · Education creates attachment; the same-day discount rewards immediate action; the follow-up WhatsApp deepens the relationship.

See EXP-03 for test design and stop rule.

Referral offer · "Trae a un primerizo"

Stack · Existing customer brings a first-time visitor; first-time visitor's first drink is free; existing customer gets a free house-made cookie with their next order.

Anchor · S/. 12–18 (free drink cost). Effective cost to Ursa · ~S/. 4–6 incremental (variable cost of drink).

Why it works · Rewards the referrer without cash; removes price friction for the new visitor; both parties have a reason to return. Hormozi's "more, better, new" applied: same product, new customer, better experience.

What the plan does not adopt from Hormozi

  • High-ticket "transformation" offers — Ursa sells cups of coffee, not coaching programs. The "transformation" framing is vulgar in hospitality.
  • Heavy discount funnels — destructive to specialty-coffee price integrity. The plan discounts only first-visit bundles and same-day cupping-related retail, never the core menu.
  • Aggressive paid-ad scaling — a single café in Miraflores does not need Facebook ad spend at Hormozi scale. Paid social is capped at S/. 1,200/month in the growth scenario.
  • "Guaranteed outcomes" language — Hormozi's claims about outcomes are his; Ursa never claims coffee will transform anyone's life.

4.7

Retention, loyalty & referral

Retention is where small cafés typically fail. The plan treats retention as the highest-leverage activity because a returning customer costs roughly one-tenth of a new one to acquire. Six mechanisms, sequenced from easy to advanced.

R1 · Loyalty punch card

Physical card, 8 punches, 9th drink free. No app required. Bear paw print per punch. Cheap, low-tech, effective for the neighborhood regular persona.

Cost: S/. 0.40/card. Stop rule: if redemption rate <15% in 60 days, replace with digital.

R2 · Bean Drop WhatsApp list

24-hour early access to new beans. Continuity without contract. See EXP-06.

Cost: S/. 0. Stop rule: per EXP-06.

R3 · Saturday cupping

Free education at the bar. Drives retail attach and Instagram follows. See EXP-03 and P-08.

Cost: 2 staff-hours/Saturday. Stop rule: per EXP-03.

R4 · Seasonal menu rotation

Every 6–8 weeks: 1–2 new beverages (named per Ursa convention), 1 new pastry, 1 new savory, 1 limited bean bag. Gives regulars a reason to return. "Summer favorites graduate to permanent" pipeline already verified in Facebook posts.

Cost: S/. 0–400/rotation (depending on recipe testing). Stop rule: if seasonal items sell <4/day, kill or rework.

R5 · "Trae a un primerizo" referral

Bring a first-time visitor, their first drink is free, referrer gets a free cookie. Word-of-mouth with a small reward. Hormozi's "free first" applied to retail hospitality.

Cost: ~S/. 4–6 incremental per redemption. Stop rule: if <5 redemptions/month after 60 days, rework reward.

R6 · Service-recovery card

Small card with the bill: "Si algo no estuvo bien, cuéntanos aquí" with WhatsApp. Catches dissatisfaction before it becomes a negative review. Sutherland principle: make it easy to complain to you, hard to complain about you.

Cost: S/. 0.05/card. Stop rule: if >3 cards/week arrive negative, escalate to owner review.

R7 · Cata Privada (paid tasting tier)

Paid private tasting for 2-person minimum, by appointment. Terrua charges US$25 (~S/. 93); Ursa undercuts at S/. 45/person with stronger value stack: 4 single origins + paired pastries + take-home 100g bag + roaster Q&A. Converts the free Saturday cupping (R3) into a pipeline — regulars who loved the free cupping self-select into the paid private tier.

Cost: 90 minutes staff time + 4 sample beans + 100g retail bag + 4 pastries ≈ S/. 22 variable cost per booking. Stop rule: if <2 bookings/month after 60 days, rework to drop-in Saturday paid tier instead.

Rory Sutherland lens — perceived-value moves that cost almost nothing

  • Name the roaster on the menu. Authorship increases perceived quality without changing the cup. Cost: S/. 0.
  • Print the roast date on every bean bag. Freshness signaling. Cost: S/. 0 (already part of roastery ops).
  • Serve filter coffee in a ceramic cup, not paper. Perceived quality doubles. Cost: ~S/. 200 one-time for cups.
  • State the altitude on the bean card. Numbers create authority. Cost: S/. 0.
  • Pour at the bar, not behind it. Visibility of craft. Cost: S/. 0 (layout decision).
  • Train baristas to introduce the origin in one sentence. Storytelling lifts perceived value. Cost: 30 minutes training.
4.8

Partnerships, tourism, hospitality & B2B

Tourism & hospitality

  • Hotel concierge sampling (EXP-05) · 8 hotels within 6 blocks. Sample bag + guide + unique QR per hotel.
  • Miraflores tourism office · If a Miraflores tourism office or visitor center exists, supply printed "Café de Miraflores" map for distribution. Cost: S/. 200 print run.
  • Hostel partnerships · Smaller hostels on Calle Diego Ferrer, Mariscal Castilla, and Alcanfores corridors. Exchange: free coffee for staff in exchange for hostel-map listing.
  • Airbnb Experience hosts · Identify 3 Lima-based Airbnb Experience hosts running food/coffee tours. Offer a free cupping in exchange for inclusion in their tour.

B2B & office

  • Office subscription pilot (EXP-08) · 5 Miraflores offices, 4-week pilot, target 2 conversions to monthly subscription.
  • WorkCafé cobrand partnership · Mirror Neira x Comunal model. Identify 1 Miraflores coworking space (Comunal Av. Benavides 871, Selina, Aetión, Impact Hub) and pitch a "WorkCafé Ursa" cobrand: Ursa supplies beans + weekly cupping at the space; coworking space promotes Ursa to members. Different from B2B subscription — this is customer-facing cobrand with shared signage.
  • Coworking spaces · Comms, Selina, WeWork if present in Miraflores. Pitch: Ursa bean + filter loan as coworking perk.
  • Agencies and studios · Design, advertising, tech studios with 10–40 employees. Pitch: weekly bean delivery + quarterly cupping at the office.
  • Restaurants · Identify 3 Miraflores restaurants serving coffee that are not specialty. Pitch Ursa wholesale beans for their post-meal coffee.

Community & events

  • Saturday cupping (EXP-03) · Anchor community event.
  • Monthly "Roaster for an hour" (P-20) · Paid, by appointment, deeper experience.
  • Bean producer visits · Once or twice a year, host a producer from a verified origin (Amazonas, Cusco, etc.) for a guided tasting. PR-worthy and brand-defining.
  • Local artist collaborations · Miraflores and Barranco artists design limited-edition bean bag labels. Revenue share or flat fee. Art-Nouveau-aligned.

Wholesale & retail

  • CoffeePass expansion · Already a partner. Negotiate featured placement in CoffeePass app for a quarter.
  • Specialty grocery · Identify 2–3 Miraflores or San Isidro specialty grocers (Wong's premium section, Tottus Hi-Lo, organic stores). Pitch Ursa retail bags.
  • Hotel minibar · For boutique hotels, offer a "Ursa in your room" single-serve drip kit. Premium price; high margin; high brand exposure.
  • Corporate gifting · December corporate-gifting season. Ursa gift basket (already verified) at S/. 80–150 price points. Pitch to Miraflores and San Isidro businesses.
4.9

Reviews, service recovery & measurement

Reviews

  • Google · Ask every regular with a positive interaction. Train staff to ask once per visit, max. Target: 5 new Google reviews/month.
  • TripAdvisor · Currently zero. Seed first 10 reviews from known regulars (no incentives, just asks). Target: 10 reviews in 60 days.
  • Rappi · Reply to every review (positive and negative) within 48 hours. Target: 100% response rate.
  • Instagram comments · Reply to every comment within 24 hours. Treats comments as micro-reviews.

Service recovery

  • In-store · Service-recovery card (R6) on every bill. WhatsApp number for direct complaints.
  • Response time · All WhatsApp messages replied within 4 hours during opening hours; within 12 hours otherwise.
  • Make-goods · Authorize staff to offer a free drink or pastry on the spot for any in-store complaint. No manager approval required up to S/. 30.
  • Documentation · Every service-recovery event logged in a shared sheet. Reviewed monthly for patterns.

Scenario selection guidance

Start with moderate. It funds the highest-ROI channels (paid Instagram, creator collaborations, hotel sampling, photography, B2B pilot) without overcommitting. Re-evaluate at day 30 against the KPI dashboard in Table 4.9. If 6 or more of 12 KPIs hit target, escalate to growth. If 4 or fewer hit, drop to lean and diagnose. Never run a channel without a metric — if a channel cannot be measured, it cannot be in the budget.

4.11

Skeptical revision pass

Skeptic 1

"Hormozi's frameworks are for online info businesses. Why apply them to a café?"

The Value Equation is category-agnostic. Every customer in every category evaluates offers along the same four variables: dream outcome, perceived likelihood, time delay, effort/sacrifice. The dossier applies the equation's logic, not Hormozi's tactical playbook. High-ticket funnels, scaling paid acquisition, and "transformation" framing — which ARE info-business-specific — are explicitly rejected in §4.4.

Skeptic 2

"Sutherland's perceived-value tricks sound like manipulation."

They are not manipulation; they are honest signaling. Naming the roaster, printing the roast date, stating the altitude, pouring at the bar — all of these make true information about Ursa's craft more visible to the customer. None of them misrepresent the product. The plan explicitly rejects the manipulation-prone variants of behavioral marketing (dark patterns, fake scarcity, urgency timers).

Skeptic 3

"The budget scenarios are guesses. The owner has no marketing budget."

That is exactly why there are three scenarios. Lean (S/. 2,500/month) is achievable by almost any operating café and uses no paid media. The plan does not assume any specific budget exists; it gives the owner a structured way to choose what to fund and what to defer. The 72-hour plan in §07 is budget-agnostic — most of its actions cost nothing.
