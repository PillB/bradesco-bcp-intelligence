   765→      <div class="ursa-card">
   766→        <h3>Skeptic 1</h3>
   767→        <p><strong>"These are HTML prototypes, not production files. How does the owner use them?"</strong></p>
   768→        <p class="muted">Each prototype is print-ready via the browser's print-to-PDF function (validated in §6.15). For social posts and Stories, screenshots at 2x resolution produce production-grade JPEGs. For packaging and printed materials, the HTML/CSS serves as a precise spec for any designer or printer — exact colors (hex), fonts (named), and dimensions (aspect ratios) are all in the source. The owner can hand a screenshot to a printer and say "match this".</p>
   769→      </div>
   770→      <div class="ursa-card">
   771→        <h4>Skeptic 2</h4>
   772→        <p><strong>"You used a generic bear in the prototypes. The real bear might look totally different."</strong></p>
   773→        <p class="muted">Correct — and stated explicitly in the asset disclaimer at the top of this module. The geometric bear used here is a placeholder concept mark for navigation and prototype demonstration. The owner-supplied asset pack (open question Q6) is required before any production artwork is finalized. Once the official bear is sourced, it replaces the concept mark in every prototype without changing the layout, palette, or typography.</p>
   774→      </div>
   775→      <div class="ursa-card">
   776→        <h4>Skeptic 3</h4>
   777→        <p><strong>"Level 3 with inverted constellation and numbered editions is too cute for a small café."</strong></p>
   778→        <p class="muted">Fair concern — which is why Level 3 is restricted to seasonal expression and explicitly recommended as optional. If the owner finds it too cute, the plan degrades gracefully: Level 1 + Level 2 alone produce a complete, distinctive system. Level 3 exists for the moment when the owner wants to celebrate a season, an anniversary, or a limited origin — never as a permanent register.</p>
